By factoring the tangent generator into low-rank factors U = [Ξ, −X] ∈ ℝD × 2K and V = [X, Ξ] ∈ ℝD × 2K, such that A = UVT, the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity decomposes the inverse into: $$\begin{equation} \label{eq:cayley_smw_formula} R_X(\Xi) = X + U \left( I_{2K} - \frac{1}{2} V^T U \right)^{-1} V^T X \end{equation}$$ The algebraic complexity reduces to 𝒪(DK2 + K3), which is purely linear in dimension D.

Proof. Direct multiplication of the block matrices yields: $$\begin{equation} V^T U = \begin{bmatrix} X^T \\ \Xi^T \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Xi & -X \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} X^T \Xi & -X^T X \\ \Xi^T \Xi & -\Xi^T X \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -I_K \\ \Xi^T \Xi & 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation}$$ Thus the kernel matrix takes the explicit block form: $$\begin{equation} M = I_{2K} - \frac{1}{2} V^T U = \begin{bmatrix} I_K & \frac{1}{2} I_K \\ -\frac{1}{2} \Xi^T \Xi & I_K \end{bmatrix} \end{equation}$$ Applying the Schur complement formula for block determinants: $$\begin{equation} \det(M) = \det(I_K) \det\left( I_K - \left(-\frac{1}{2} \Xi^T \Xi\right) I_K^{-1} \left(\frac{1}{2} I_K\right) \right) = \det\left( I_K + \frac{1}{4} \Xi^T \Xi \right) \end{equation}$$ Since ΞTΞ is symmetric positive semi-definite with non-negative eigenvalues σj2 ≥ 0, $\det(M) = \prod_{j=1}^K (1 + \frac{1}{4} \sigma_j^2) \ge 1$, proving M is strictly nonsingular for any tangent step without regularization. ◻
